# Source-backed resume quality

Use this reference whenever generating, revising, or reviewing a Base Resume
or JD Variant.

## Decide what deserves emphasis

Rank candidate facts by:

1. relevance to the target category or verified JD requirement;
2. demonstrated outcome or change, not responsibility alone;
3. personal ownership and decision scope;
4. evidence strength and specificity;
5. differentiation from a typical candidate.

Mark only the best 3–6 claims as `core`. Put core claims early in the summary,
experience, or project where a scanning reader can find them. Mark the rest as
`supporting`. More highlighted claims do not create more focus.

## Write a resume point

Prefer this sequence:

`strong action + problem/project and scope + personal approach or decision + result`

Include a metric only when a source supports it. A concrete non-numeric result,
technical constraint, shipped capability, adoption signal, or verified scope is
better than an invented percentage. Remove generic adjectives such as
"excellent" or "proficient" unless the surrounding evidence demonstrates them.

Keep one primary achievement per bullet. Put the strongest and most relevant
information first. Use skills in context inside an achievement instead of
relying on an unsupported skill list.

## Build the evidence record

For every rendered professional-summary paragraph, capability line, and bullet:

- copy the exact rendered text into `claim`;
- set `importance` to `core` or `supporting`;
- explain the hiring signal in `why_it_matters`;
- attach one or more stable source locators;
- paraphrase in each source's `evidence` field exactly what that source proves;
- mark `needs_review` when authorship, scope, result, or attribution is uncertain;
- write `improvement` when the point needs a stronger verb, clearer ownership,
  missing scope, missing result, or stronger source.

A locator is not an evidence explanation. A JD proves employer demand, not the
candidate's ability. Do not let the JD serve as a candidate source.

## Derive interview questions from each point

Add 1–4 questions that test the point rather than repeat it. Cover the most
useful angles among:

- ownership: what the candidate personally decided and implemented;
- depth: how the implementation works and where the difficult boundary was;
- tradeoff: alternatives considered and why this choice won;
- result: how the outcome was measured or otherwise observed;
- failure: what broke, what changed, and how risk was controlled;
- transfer: how the experience applies to the target role.

Use the same sources to prepare a truthful STAR/PAR story. Do not put the answer
into the question. A core point should normally have at least one depth or
tradeoff question and one outcome or failure question.

## Authoritative guidance used for this protocol

- Harvard Mignone Center: make resumes specific, active, fact-based, easy to
  scan, tailored, and results-oriented:
  https://careerservices.fas.harvard.edu/resources/create-a-strong-resume/
- MIT CAPD: use Project/Action/Result statements, start with action verbs, and
  quantify outcomes when possible:
  https://capd.mit.edu/resources/career-toolkit-crafting-an-effective-resume/
- Yale OCS: connect Action + Project + Result and ask for tangible evidence of
  accomplishments:
  https://ocs.yale.edu/resources/writing-impactful-resume-bullets/
- MIT CAPD: prepare truthful, specific STAR stories from job-relevant skills
  and experiences:
  https://capd.mit.edu/resources/the-star-method-for-behavioral-interviews/
